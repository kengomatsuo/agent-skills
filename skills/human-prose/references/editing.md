# Editing a draft so it reads as a person's

Written 2026-11-05 from the LAMP study of professional editors (CHI 2025), Wikipedia's signs of
human writing, Reinhart et al. (PNAS 2025) and the copywriting sources in `sources.md`.

## Contents

- The method
- Before and after
- When a fix makes a new tell

## The method

1. **Find the reader's question for each paragraph.** If you cannot say what the paragraph answers,
   cut it. LAMP's editors cut "unnecessary or redundant exposition" more than anything else.
2. **Find the fact.** For every claim, ask what number, name, time, price or event it rests on. Put
   that fact in the sentence. If the writer has not given you one, ask for it, or mark the line as a
   placeholder. Inventing a fact is worse than leaving the line generic.
3. **Rewrite the sentence from the fact.** Start from who does what. Do not edit the old sentence
   word by word, because its shape survives a word swap.
4. **Join what belongs together.** If three short sentences carry one idea, make them one sentence
   with "because", "so", "when" or "which". If one sentence carries two ideas, split it.
5. **Check the page as a whole.** Read the sentence openings down the left margin: if many start
   the same way, or every paragraph ends on a summing-up line, rework those. Then read it aloud.

## Before and after

Each "before" is quoted from a real page or study (fetched 2026-11-05). Each "after" uses only facts
the same source states, or is marked where a fact has to come from the writer.

**1. A line any rival could print (7shifts, https://7shifts.com)**

- Before: "Everything your team relies on, working together"
- After: "Scheduling, payroll and tips for restaurant teams, in one app."
- Why: the facts come from 7shifts' own meta description. The heading said nothing a reader could
  check; the rewrite names the three jobs it does.

**2. Praise with nothing under it (Restaurant365 meta description, https://www.restaurant365.com)**

- Before: "helps leaders master accounting, operations, & workforce to create incredible moments
  that drive profits"
- A real line that does the job (MarginEdge, https://www.marginedge.com): "See a daily P&L and get
  alerted when prices on your most-used products change."
- Why: the MarginEdge line says what the owner sees and when. "Incredible moments" is a benefit no
  one can measure.

**3. Noun stack and participle tail (Llama 3 70B output quoted in Reinhart et al.)**

- Before: "These schemes can help to reduce deforestation, habitat destruction, and pollution, while
  also promoting sustainable consumption patterns."
- After (needs the writer's fact): "Farmers in the scheme cleared [N] fewer hectares last year."
- Why: four nominalizations and a "while also" tail, and no actor. The fix needs a subject doing a
  verb, and the number has to come from the writer.

**4. The clipped page the old version of this skill produced (house draft, stya, 2026-10)**

- Before: "Stya prints the order. Then it takes payment. No waiting. No lost tickets."
- After: "When the waiter taps Send, the order prints in the kitchen straight away, so the cook
  starts before anyone has paid."
- Why: four short sentences, a negative run and no connective is the GPT-6-era rhythm. One sentence
  with "when" and "so" says the same thing the way a person explains it.

**5. Indonesian, generic (Moka, https://www.mokapos.com)**

- Before: "Satu Aplikasi POS untuk Semua Kebutuhan Bisnis"
- After: "Cek penjualan dan stok kamu dari satu aplikasi kasir."
- Why: "semua kebutuhan bisnis" claims everything and names nothing. Moka's own description
  mentions sales reports and inventory, so the rewrite names those.

**6. What human product prose sounds like (Fathom, https://usefathom.com)**

- "For the longest time, website analytics software was seriously bad. It was hard to understand,
  time-consuming to use, and worse, it exploited visitor data for big tech to profit. We built
  Fathom to make analytics easy to use and respectful of privacy."
- Why it reads as a person: "seriously bad" is how someone talks, "and worse" is a spoken aside, the
  sentences run 10, 23 and 15 words, and "we built" names who did it. It even has a three-item list,
  and it reads fine because the three complaints are real.

**7. A button (Copyhackers, https://copyhackers.com/2014/09/buttons-vs-headlines/)**

- Before: "Sign up now"
- After: "Show me outfits I'll love"
- Why: the reader's own goal, in their words. Clicks rose 123.9% in Dressipi's test.

## When a fix makes a new tell

- Cutting long sentences to short ones makes the 8 to 20 word band. Rejoin by meaning.
- Removing every dash leaves only periods and commas. Use a parenthesis, a colon before a list, or a
  dash where it is the natural mark.
- Swapping a flagged word for a synonym keeps the cluster. Rewrite from the fact.
- Cutting a list of three to two leaves a run of pairs. Keep as many items as there are.
- Removing every adjective and adverb leaves flat, telegraphic copy. Harry Dry's own rule is that
  adjectives are fine and ideas should not start with them.
