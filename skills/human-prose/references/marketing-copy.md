## Before writing

Collect the reader's words. Design Academy wrote its landing page from sign-up answers to "what is
your biggest frustration with design?", and Copyhackers rewrote Dressipi's headline from words
prospects used in forums. For a restaurant product, that means what owners and staff say at the
till, in WhatsApp groups, in support chats and in reviews. Write down five to ten of their phrases
before writing a line, and decide how much the visitor already knows: someone who searched "aplikasi
kasir restoran" knows the category, while someone who clicked an ad may not know they have the
problem (Schwartz's awareness levels, via Copyhackers).

## Headline

Ogilvy: "five times as many people read the headline as read the body copy." The headline says what
the product is or does for this reader, specifically enough that only this product could say it.

- Say the job in the reader's words: "Screen sharing for people who build software." (Tuple)
- Or name the difference the reader cares about: "Analytics without the cookies" (Fathom), "Super
  simple group chat, without a subscription." (Campfire)
- Or name the problem as the reader feels it: "Food costs jumped 3% and you don't know why?"
  (MarginEdge)
- Avoid category-wide lines such as "Cut the chaos with restaurant essentials" (Square) or "A new
  species of product tool" (Linear). If a competitor could put it on their page unchanged, rewrite.
- Use the words a visitor searched for. Copyhackers matches the headline to what the visitor
  expected when they clicked.

## Subhead

The subhead answers the question the headline raises, usually how, for whom, or at what cost. It is
a full sentence and may be long: "Gmail, Outlook, and Apple got complacent and took their eye off the
ball. Then along came HEY." Put the checkable facts here if the headline has none (price, platforms,
time to set up).

## Feature lines

Each feature line says what the reader can now do or stop doing, then the fact that makes it true.

- "Never enter line item details again." then "Connect your supplier or snap pictures of your …
  invoices." (Orderly)
- "The first time someone emails you they land in The Screener." (HEY): describes the screen,
  which is more convincing than an adjective.
- "It just takes one click to switch who's sharing." (Tuple)

Write features from the reader's side ("you see a daily P&L") and give each one its own fact.
Harry Dry: "No one cares what you can do. Everyone cares what you can do for them." Avoid the words
he calls landing-page words (unlock, unleash, empower, supercharge, enhance, seamless) because real
people do not say them, and because the right fix is to name the outcome, not to find a synonym.

Contractions, "you", sentences that start with And or But, and a joke where it fits are all normal
in good copy (Dry's conversational guide; HEY's "Calendaring sucked for years, we fixed it"). The
house voice for a given product (its brand skill) sets how far to go.

## Buttons and calls to action

Name what the reader gets when they press it, in their words. "Show me outfits I'll love" beat "Sign
up now" by 124% in clicks (Copyhackers). For software: "Start a 14-day trial", "See the menu on your
phone", "Book a 15-minute demo". Put the cost of pressing beside the button when it is low: "7-day
free trial · From $15/month · Cancel anytime" (Fathom).

## Proof, pricing and FAQ

- Quote customers by name and venue, with the specific thing that changed. "Orderly is such a crucial
  part of keeping us afloat" is weaker than a quote with a number in it.
- Show the price. "Transparent pricing. Really." (MarginEdge) only works because the price is shown
  under it.
- Answer FAQs in the voice of the person who asked: Campfire answers "Can I host it myself?" with
  "Absolutely. Wherever you can host something like WordPress, you can host Campfire."
- A promise needs its terms: Buttondown says "we'll get you migrated within one business day".

## Lines from real pages

Quoted 2026-11-05, under 15 words each.

| Works because it is specific | Reads as anyone's |
|---|---|
| "Analytics without the cookies" (usefathom.com) | "Cut the chaos with restaurant essentials" (squareup.com) |
| "We sell software, not data" (usefathom.com) | "Everything your team relies on, working together" (7shifts.com) |
| "AI runs the numbers. You run the restaurant." (marginedge.com) | "Fuel smarter upsells and better experiences with guest data" (sevenrooms.com) |
| "Never enter line item details again." (getorderly.com) | "A new species of product tool." (linear.app) |
| "Rooms can be private to some, or open to everyone" (once.com/campfire) | "Satu Aplikasi POS untuk Semua Kebutuhan Bisnis" (mokapos.com) |
| "we'll get you migrated within one business day" (buttondown.com) | "Ekosistem Terlengkap & Terintegrasi untuk F&B" (esb.id) |

Two of the specific lines use a contrast ("not data", "You run the restaurant"). A contrast is fine
once, when the two halves are both concrete; the tell is a page built from them.
