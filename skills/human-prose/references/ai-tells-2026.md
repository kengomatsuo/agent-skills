## How to use this file

A tell is a reason to reread a sentence. Readers react to clusters, so one hit proves nothing and
four on a page usually means the page was not written for its reader. Fix a hit by rewriting the
sentence from its fact (`editing.md`). Replacing the word with a synonym leaves the shape the
reader noticed, and Wikipedia's editors say outright that synonyms of a flagged word are not
themselves overused.

Tells move with each model. "Delve" was everywhere in 2023 and had nearly gone by 2025. Check the
era of the text before reading too much into an old word.

| Grok | causal, empirical, correlate, underscore |

Claude also gives objects human verbs: a check that "bites", a rule that "holds", a number that
"earns its place". When the subject is a thing, ask who does the action and make that person or
screen the subject, or use "is".

## Sentence shapes

Each of these is ordinary rhetoric. The tell is finding the same one in paragraph after
paragraph.

- **Corrective contrast.** "It's not just a till, it's a way of running the shop." "Y rather than
  X." "Not simply…". Write what the thing is and does.
- **Three of everything.** "Fast, simple and reliable." Wikipedia notes the sign is strongest
  where nobody would bother with a flourish. Keep a list at the number of things there really are.
- **Label, colon, sentence.** "The fix: …", "Short version: …", "The catch: …". Write the sentence.
- **Fragment opener.** "One caution." "The difference?" followed by the point. Start with the
  point.
- **Negative run.** "No setup. No contracts. No surprises." Say what the reader gets.
- **Participle tail.** "…, ensuring every order is tracked." Make it a sentence with its own
  subject, or cut it.
- **"Serves as", "stands as", "features", "boasts", "offers"** where "is" or "has" is meant.
- **Noun stacks.** "Real-time inventory-driven cost visibility." Unpack into who sees what, when.
- **The coda.** A final line that restates the page or reaches for a moral ("In the end, a good
  till is about people."). Delete it.

## Inflation and empty claims

- Significance with no fact: "marks a pivotal moment", "plays a key role", "a testament to".
- Vague authority: "experts agree", "studies show" with no name.
- Benefits with no cost or number: "seamless", "effortless", "powerful". Wikipedia notes newer
  models praise quietly instead of loudly, so look for a benefit nobody could check.
- "Matters because", "the key is", "what really matters": state the consequence itself.
- Synonym cycling: the dashboard, the interface, the portal, the hub for one screen.
- An ad-like sensory phrase inside plain information ("the warm hum of a busy dining room" in a
  paragraph about pricing).

## Rhythm and punctuation

- **Uniform sentences.** Wikipedia describes GPT-6-era text as "much more period and comma usage
  and shorter sentences around a strict 8–20 word limit". A whole page in that band reads as
  machine.
- **Punctuation monotony.** After vendors suppressed the em dash, model text uses only periods and
  commas, more than people do. People also use parentheses, colons, semicolons, question marks and
  the occasional dash.
- **Dash density.** Claude still uses more em dashes than professional writers (July 2026 study via
  Wikipedia). Several spaced dashes in one paragraph is the old tell; one dash where it fits is
  ordinary.
- **Staccato drama.** Four or more very short sentences in a row building to a reveal.

## Formatting

Title case headings, bold text scattered through prose, a bold label at the start of every bullet,
emoji in headings or bullets, a heading over every two-sentence section, curly quotes in a context
that otherwise uses straight ones, and Markdown in places that do not render it.

## Reasoning and conversation left on the page

"I hope this helps", "Great question", "Let me be precise", "So, to answer your question", "Here's
the thing", notes about what changed since the last draft, and a page addressed to the person who
asked for it instead of its reader.

## Not tells

Wikipedia lists these as signs of human writing or as ineffective indicators. Keep them where a
person would use them:

- "is", "has", "there is"; plain verbs such as wrote, moved, used, tried.
- "very", "perhaps", "tends to", "in order to", "the fact that".
- A true superlative: "one of the best", "the only", "the first".
- Perfect grammar, a mix of casual and formal register, formal or academic words in general.
- A transition word on its own (however, also, so).
